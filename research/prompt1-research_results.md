# Embedded firmware + test & integration interviews — what your research missed

## TL;DR
- **Your existing research is mostly right but mis-attributed and stale in three specific places**: the "~50% of RTOS candidates fail mutex-vs-semaphore" figure is **Niall Cooling / Feabhas**, not Barr; the Tesla broken-code take-home is confirmed verbatim; and the **embedded system-design round is real, dedicated, and now spreading beyond FAANG to Medtronic** — and you have no mechanism for it.
- **The single biggest hole is Task 2b**: a from-scratch, 45-minute "architect this embedded subsystem out loud" round exists at Google and Meta Reality Labs and increasingly appears as a take-home design item at Tesla and Medtronic. It grades ISR/main partitioning, RAM/flash budgeting, comms topology, power states, failure handling, and testability — none of which your 83 capabilities exercise. I propose **E30** to close it, plus **E29, E31–E33, H11, T24–T25, B11**.
- **For a Canada-based web→hardware switcher, your leverage is the project deep-dive** (Rocket Lab, SpaceX, Anduril, Tesla all center a round on one project you defend on constraint/trade-off/test grounds — your STM32 robot *is* the interview). The killers are live-coding syntax freeze under observation and inability to reason about hardware/datasheets/bench instruments. Note a hard gate: **Anduril and most US defense-space roles require U.S. Person status** (citizen or lawful permanent resident) under ITAR/EAR.

## Key Findings

1. **The embedded system-design round is a genuine, gradable round — and under-resourced publicly.** Multiple first-hand Blind reports confirm Google runs a dedicated "embedded system design" round plus an "embedded domain" round; Meta Reality Labs runs an in-domain design round that has rejected otherwise-strong candidates. Real prompts, not invented: "buffered stream with incoming/periodic-outgoing data while an ISR operates on it" (Tesla take-home), "implement your own malloc with a memory pool / implement a timer library / producer-consumer" (Meta RL), "FIFOs and multi-core synchronization" (Meta RL), and "design a firmware logging/telemetry subsystem for an implantable device with limited flash uploading over BLE" (Medtronic). It differs from web system-design in scope (one device/subsystem, not distributed scale) and axes (RAM/flash/power/ISR timing/peripheral protocols, not sharding/availability).

2. **Tesla's "debug code you did not write" take-home is confirmed verbatim** and you have no exercise for it. Blind: "For Firmware, 90min/2hr take home coding challenge… not LC but very typical embedded questions like system design, state machine, debugging code, bit masking and manipulation." A separate Tesla Glassdoor report (Dec 2024): "Was given code written in c and asked to find any errors." Intel runs a dedicated "Debug/Arch" round. **No source enumerates the exact planted bugs** — treat any bug taxonomy (missing `volatile`, off-by-one, ISR reentrancy, TX-IRQ storm, uninitialized memory) as inferred from domain knowledge.

3. **Mutex-vs-semaphore attribution corrected.** The named authority and the "~half fail it" figure is Niall Cooling (Chartered Engineer, Founder of Feabhas), on EmbeddedRelated: *"The difference between a semaphore and mutex has been one of my go-to interview questions for anyone claiming RTOS expertise. Easily 50% make the classic mistake pointed out in this article!"* Barr Group's "Mutexes and Semaphores Demystified" is a supporting primary source (ownership is the distinguishing property), but the interview-fail statistic is Cooling's, not Barr's — fix your deck.

4. **Web→hardware is received as harder than the reverse, and hardware fluency is the gate.** A working engineer on Blind: *"Embedded is a very different animal… It's way more than simply picking up C. You have to understand hardware… debug live in an electronics lab with all sorts of equipment (oscilloscopes, logic analyzers)… You're a bridge between software and hardware."* The recurring rejection story is real and first-hand: *"I have worked close to 10 years delivering firmware… Was rejected by a FAANG like company, and they said I didn't know how to code… a recent college graduate interviewed me."*

5. **Newer-field weighting was under-called.** Rust reached **48.8% of organizations making non-trivial use in 2025** (2025 State of Rust Survey, The Rust Blog), up 10.1 points in two years, and Zephyr is now a standing job category — but at junior level both remain "nice to have," not filters. Functional safety (ISO 26262 ASIL, DO-178C DAL) and medical cybersecurity are real, dated, and absent from your spec: **MC/DC coverage is required at DO-178C Software Level A and ISO 26262 ASIL D**; the FDA premarket cyber mandate is **FD&C Act §524B**, effective March 29, 2023, with FDA issuing "Refuse to Accept" letters for non-compliant cyber-device submissions since October 1, 2023.

### SECTION 2 — New deck cards

#### rtos
rtos ||| When would you choose bare-metal or a super-loop over an RTOS, and when does an RTOS earn its keep? ||| An RTOS earns its keep with multiple independent activities at different rates/priorities that would tangle a super-loop, when you need blocking APIs and priority-based preemption, or when middleware (BLE, USB, TCP) assumes threads. Bare-metal/super-loop wins when timing is simple, RAM is tiny, and you want full determinism with no scheduler overhead. Name the cost: an RTOS adds per-task stack RAM, context-switch latency, and new failure modes. ||| Saying "always use an RTOS, it's more professional" or being unable to name one concrete RTOS cost (per-task stack RAM, priority inversion, larger bug surface). ||| E17 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/1-Describe-the-difference-between-a-semaphore-and-a-mutex-and-when-you-might-use-each-2-when-would-you-use-an-RTOS-over-QTN_1740523.htm
rtos ||| Design your own cooperative task scheduler for an MCU with no OS. What are the data structures? ||| A table of task control blocks each holding a function pointer, a period, and a next-run timestamp; a non-blocking loop that compares a free-running millis counter (rollover-safe subtraction) against each task's next-run and dispatches due tasks. Tasks run to completion and never block. Discuss jitter, a long task starving others, and how you'd add priorities. ||| Using blocking delay() inside tasks, or using 'if (now > next_run)' which breaks at timer rollover instead of unsigned '(now - next_run)' comparison. ||| E23,E17 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.teamblind.com/post/firmware-interview-full-loop-at-meta-reality-labs-uqmfofhd
rtos ||| What does task priority actually get you, and what does it NOT get you? ||| Priority decides which ready task runs when several are ready — ordering, not proportional CPU share. A high-priority task that never blocks starves everything below it. Priority does not prevent priority inversion and does not guarantee a deadline unless the full schedule is analyzed. ||| "Higher priority means it runs faster / gets more CPU time." ||| E13,E17 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.embeddedrelated.com/showarticle/1248.php

#### sync
sync ||| A candidate says "a mutex is just a semaphore initialized to one." What is wrong with that? ||| A mutex has ownership: only the task that took it may give it, enabling priority inheritance to bound priority inversion. A binary semaphore has no owner — any task or ISR can give it — so it is a signaling primitive, not a locking one. Using a semaphore where you need a mutex loses priority inheritance and lets the wrong context release the lock. ||| "They're basically the same, semaphores just count higher than one" — the exact answer Niall Cooling (Founder, Feabhas) reports "Easily 50%" of self-described RTOS candidates give. ||| E12 ||| HIGH ||| https://www.embeddedrelated.com/showarticle/1248.php
sync ||| Can you give (signal) a semaphore from an ISR? Can you take a mutex from an ISR? ||| You can give/release a semaphore from an ISR (FromISR API + yield-request pattern) — the canonical "defer work to a task" mechanism. You must never take a mutex from an ISR: an ISR has no thread identity to own it, and blocking in an ISR is forbidden. ||| "You can't touch any RTOS primitive from an ISR" (too broad — signaling FromISR is standard) or that taking a mutex in an ISR is fine. ||| E12,E9 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://barrgroup.com/blog/mutexes-and-semaphores-demystified-0

#### protocols
protocols ||| Two I2C devices could talk on the same bus. How does I2C arbitrate, and how do you design around a conflict? ||| I2C uses open-drain lines and multi-master arbitration by wired-AND: a master driving a 1 but reading a 0 has lost arbitration and backs off; addressing plus clock synchronization keep it coherent. If two slaves share a fixed address you can't fix it on one bus — use an address-select pin, an I2C mux/switch, or a second bus. ||| "Conflicts are impossible because each device has a unique address," ignoring identical fixed-address parts and multi-master arbitration. ||| E19 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.teamblind.com/post/embedded-system-design-interview-questions-8vye2v1m
protocols ||| Design an interrupt-driven UART driver with a ring buffer. Walk the RX and TX data path. ||| On RX, the interrupt fires per byte/FIFO threshold, the ISR pushes into a ring buffer and returns fast; main drains it. On TX, main fills the buffer and enables the TX-empty interrupt; the ISR pops until empty, then disables the TX interrupt. Discuss overflow policy, head/tail ownership across ISR/main, flow control. ||| Doing framing/parsing inside the ISR, or leaving the TX-empty interrupt enabled after drain, causing a continuous interrupt storm. ||| E10,C5 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://medium.com/@akashagrawal_33749/cracking-the-firmware-embedded-systems-engineer-interview-d73a37da95bd

#### memory
memory ||| Implement your own malloc/free with a fixed memory pool for an MCU. What must you get right? ||| Maintain a free list; on alloc find a block and split; on free return and coalesce adjacent free blocks to fight fragmentation. Enforce worst-case alignment, handle exhaustion by returning NULL (never trap), detect double-free. On an MCU prefer fixed-block pools for O(1) alloc and zero fragmentation. ||| Ignoring fragmentation/coalescing, or not naming determinism/fragmentation as *why* firmware avoids the heap on a device running a year. ||| C9,E5 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.teamblind.com/post/firmware-interview-full-loop-at-meta-reality-labs-uqmfofhd
memory ||| Design an aligned allocator: return a block aligned to N bytes from an unaligned pool. ||| Over-allocate by (size + alignment − 1 + bookkeeping), round the pointer up to the boundary with a mask, and store the original pointer just below the aligned address so free() can recover it. Alignment must be a power of two for the mask to work. ||| Rounding with modulo/division on an MCU, forgetting to stash the base pointer, or assuming malloc already returns your required alignment. ||| C9 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.teamblind.com/post/system-design-interview-questions-for-embedded-software-developer-44wqfhfy

#### debug
debug ||| I hand you a working firmware binary and a non-working one for the same board; no obvious source diff. How do you find the difference? ||| Diff the disassembly, dump and diff every peripheral register at the fault point, check the linker map for placement differences, and bisect compiler flags/optimization levels. Verify bottom-up: clocks, power, peripheral init order. The bug is often init ordering or an optimizer exposing a missing volatile. ||| Rewriting code or blaming the toolchain without diffing register state and disassembly; assuming the two builds "should be identical" and not checking optimization level. ||| H1,E1 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://dev.to/embedsys/embedded-systems-weekly-114-812
debug ||| You are handed a broken codebase in the take-home and told "make the tests pass." How do you approach code you did not write? ||| Read the entry point and data flow first, run it, and reproduce the failure before changing anything. Instrument at seams, form one hypothesis at a time, fix the smallest thing that turns a test green. Look first at the embedded-classic planted bugs: missing volatile on ISR-shared data, off-by-one buffer indices, sign/shift bugs, uninitialized memory, reentrancy. ||| Rewriting whole functions from scratch instead of localizing the defect, or changing several things at once so you cannot attribute the fix. ||| H1,C1 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.teamblind.com/post/tesla-embedded-software-firmware-interview-process-vtm3gmrw

#### state machines
state machines ||| Design the firmware for a vending machine as a state machine: coin inserted, product selected, cancel, dispense. ||| Enumerate states (idle, collecting, sufficient-funds, dispensing, refunding) and a transition table keyed by (state, event). Keep a running balance, handle cancel/refund and exact-change edge cases, make dispense idempotent against double-events. Separate pure transition logic (host-testable) from I/O drivers. ||| Building it as nested if/else on globals with no explicit state enum, so cancel-mid-transaction and double-coin events corrupt the balance. ||| E23 ||| HIGH ||| https://www.teamblind.com/post/tesla-embedded-software-firmware-interview-process-vtm3gmrw

#### timing
timing ||| Given one hardware timer, build an API that supports an arbitrary number of software timers. ||| Keep a sorted list/min-heap of software timers by absolute expiry; program the single hardware timer to the nearest expiry; on interrupt fire all expired entries, reschedule periodics, and re-arm to the next. Use rollover-safe unsigned comparisons and keep ISR work minimal. ||| One hardware timer per software timer (doesn't scale), busy-polling all timers each tick, or ignoring rollover in the expiry compare. ||| E24,C11 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.teamblind.com/post/system-design-interview-questions-for-embedded-software-developer-44wqfhfy

#### static
static ||| What is functional safety, and how does ASIL (ISO 26262) or DAL (DO-178C) change how you write and test code? ||| The integrity level is derived from a hazard/risk analysis (ASIL A–D automotive; DAL A–E avionics) and dictates rigor: required structural coverage, requirement-to-code-to-test traceability, tool qualification, and review independence. At the top level (DO-178C Level A / ISO 26262 ASIL D) MC/DC structural coverage is required. Higher level means more redundancy and objective evidence, not just "more tests." ||| Treating the standards as "write more tests" or documentation formality, and not knowing the level comes from a hazard analysis and drives coverage type (e.g., MC/DC at the top level). ||| T4,T23 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://interviewprep.org/iso-26262-interview-questions/
static ||| What does MISRA-C actually buy you, and what are its limits? ||| MISRA-C removes undefined/implementation-defined and error-prone C constructs (implicit conversions, some pointer arithmetic, dynamic memory) so defects are prevented and code is analyzable; deviations must be documented, and analyzers enforce it. Its limit: it constrains constructs, it does not prove functional correctness or catch logic errors. ||| Claiming MISRA "makes code safe" or "finds bugs" — it prevents construct-level defects and aids analysis; it does not verify behavior. ||| T23 ||| MEDIUM ||| https://www.tustinrecruiting.com/interview-prep/technical/embedded-systems/automotive-embedded-engineer-interview-questions-and-answers

#### interrupts
interrupts ||| An ISR shares a multi-byte struct (timestamp + value) with main, and volatile is on it. Why can this still be wrong? ||| volatile forces re-reads; it does not make a multi-word access atomic. Main can read a half-updated struct if an interrupt lands mid-copy. You need a critical section (briefly disable the interrupt), a double-buffer/seqlock, or a lock-free SPSC ring to get a consistent snapshot. ||| "volatile makes it safe/atomic" — volatile is about visibility, not atomicity or tearing across words. ||| E10,E16 ||| HIGH ||| https://www.teamblind.com/post/embedded-swfirmware-interviews-qet5hwmy

#### test infra
test infra ||| Design a CI pipeline that runs tests against real hardware. #### v&v
v&v ||| For an implantable/safety-critical device, how do you decide test rigor per function? ||| Drive rigor from a hazard analysis: classify each function by safety impact (IEC 62304 class A/B/C, or ASIL/DAL), then apply proportional verification — higher class demands unit + integration + system tests, structural coverage, and full requirement-to-test traceability with reviews. #### process
process ||| What is secure boot and why does it matter for a networked medical or space device? ||| Secure boot verifies a cryptographic signature over the firmware image before executing it, so only authenticated code runs; combined with a hardware root of trust and anti-rollback it blocks tampered/downgraded firmware. ## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Close the two known holes first (weeks 1–3).** Build the **E30 embedded-system-design mechanism** and the **"debug code you did not write"** kata immediately; these are your highest-value gaps and each is confirmed by a real, dated round. For E30, drill the six-axis rubric out loud on the 4c prompts: partition ISR/main, budget RAM/flash, pick comms topology, plan power states, state failure handling, and name how you'd test it. Practice the "functional → fast → scalable" ordering and *ask clarifying questions first* — the automatic-failure pattern (proposing before requirements) that already governs your test-design rubric applies here too. **Benchmark to advance:** you can produce a labeled block diagram and survive one round of interviewer pushback within 45 minutes on three different prompts.

**Stage 2 — Fix attribution and add the safety/security cards (week 3–4).** Correct the mutex/semaphore card to credit Niall Cooling/Feabhas. Add E32/E33 (functional safety + medical cyber) as MEDIUM/LOW cards scoped to automotive/aero and connected-medical targets only — don't drill them for subsea/research robotics.

**Stage 3 — Weight prep by target domain (ongoing).** For your primary Canada targets (space, subsea, medical, research robotics), the pattern is **project-presentation + resume-deep-dive + practical technical**, not LeetCode. Invest disproportionately in B10/B3/B4 (the 10-minute presentation and constraint→trade-off→test narrative) and in H-track bench/hardware fluency. Keep the thin LeetCode-in-C track alive only for Google/Meta/Anduril-style pipelines.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** If you start getting US-defense-space interviews (Anduril, some SpaceX/Blue Origin roles), stop — verify U.S. Person eligibility before investing prep; ITAR/EAR is a hard gate for a Canadian without permanent US residency. If a target company's take-home ships an automated test suite (Tesla, SpaceX), shift practice toward *making red tests green under time pressure* rather than greenfield authoring. If Rust or Zephyr appears as *required* (not "nice to have") in a specific posting, add a focused module; at junior level in 2026 it is still rarely a filter.

## Caveats
- **First-hand vs. policy:** Everything sourced to Blind/Glassdoor/blogs is "candidates report," never company policy. Company processes change quietly and vary by org and team within one company (e.g., SpaceX Starlink vs flight software differ).
- **Inferred content is marked:** planted-bug taxonomies, the neat six-axis design rubric, and several "trap" columns are inferred from domain knowledge where no source states them explicitly; they are labeled MEDIUM/LOW or INFERRED accordingly.
- **Older sources:** the Cooling/Feabhas article (2018) and parts of the Agrawal blog (2021) are older than two years; their claims are corroborated by current reports where possible but the specific numbers (e.g., "50%") are single-author.
- **Aggregator caution:** ISO 26262, HIL/CAN, and PyVISA/SCPI content came from prep-site aggregators used only for *themes*, not for specific claims; the underlying standards (DO-178C MC/DC, FD&C §524B) are grounded in primary regulatory sources.
- **Coverage gap:** medical/space/robotics T&I interview reporting is genuinely thin — Medtronic and Intuitive are the only two with usable dated first-hand test-role detail, and even those lean behavioral. Don't over-fit your T&I deck to two companies.
