## Recommendations

**Stage 1 - now, because your live-Python weakness is the binding constraint for Camp A.** Drill the P1/P2/P3 capabilities to their evidence bars before applying to Tesla/SpaceX/Qualcomm/Rocket Lab. Concretely: (a) run the 12 new Python/bytes deck cards as flashcards until you can give the "why" cold; (b) run the six new Python coding exercises (stream-parse, TLV decode, endianness-bug, frame reassembly, CLI-by-serial-number, retry decorator) on a timer, logging clean-first-run rate exactly as you do for C. **Benchmark that changes the plan:** if after 20 logged reps you are below 15/20 on P3 (domain scripting), do NOT yet apply to Tesla-type live-Python roles - you will burn a real interview slot; prioritise Camp B (Medtronic/Abbott/Intuitive) instead, where the live-Python bar is lower.

**Stage 2 - in parallel, exploit where your background actually transfers.** Your Docker/CI/CD/pytest experience maps cleanly to the mid-level "own and design the framework, integrate CI/CD" language. Make your HIL harness demonstrate exactly that: real hardware in the loop, results logged against a firmware build hash, GitHub Actions running it on a self-hosted runner, a clean README with a one-paragraph "what this tests and why" summary, and disciplined commit messages. Build it so it survives the four "fake automation" questions (how many tests, how long to run, how often, how run). **Threshold:** if a reviewer cannot understand the project's value in 30 seconds from the README, it is not done.

**Stage 3 - target sequencing given your constraints.** As a Canadian citizen, Rocket Lab US, SpaceX, Blue Origin and Northrop are gated by US-person/ITAR requirements (Rocket Lab's own posting says "US citizenship is required"); treat them as secondary/aspirational, not primary. Prioritise: (1) Kraken Robotics (Canada, subsea, already known to give a Python + technical-writing take-home you can prepare for directly), (2) Canadian Medtronic/Abbott sites (Camp B, lower live-Python bar, your Docker/CI transfers, salary floor achievable), (3) Tesla/Qualcomm Canada or remote (Camp A - only after Stage 1 benchmark passes). **Add T27 (read C#) only if** you get an Intuitive or Medtronic manufacturing-test interview; do not invest in LabVIEW/TestStand at all unless a specific target role names it.

**Stage 4 - fix the AI-shallowness directly.** The industry is moving to audit AI use (BrightSign requires a PROMPTS.md), not ban it - so the winning move is to be able to explain and defend generated code live. Practice every kata twice: once with AI, then once cold, and only count the cold rep toward your logged rate.

## Caveats

- **Two-camp generalisation is directional, not absolute.** Individual teams vary; a Medtronic automation team can be more Python-live than the camp average, and a Tesla thermal-validation role can be lighter on coding than a firmware-QA one. Match prep to the specific job description, not the camp label.
- **The 32-posting sample is curated, not random.** It over-represents Python/PyVISA roles because those are the queries that surfaced results; the frequency table is directional (confidence MEDIUM at best). Do not quote "28 of 32" as a market statistic.
- **Candidate reports vs company policy.** Every Glassdoor/Blind item is a candidate self-report, not company policy - the Tesla 90-minute Python test and Qualcomm live round are "candidates report", stated as such. Aggregator guides (Dataford, InterviewQuery, TechPrep) are synthesised and used only for themes.
- **INFERRED items are flagged in-field.** Several deck traps and the HIL failure modes are reasoned, not sourced; they carry source-URL INFERRED. Test them against a real reviewer before treating them as gospel.
- **Binary-parsing-in-interviews is inferred from take-home descriptions and daily-work postings,** not from a first-person "they asked me to unpack this struct" report; the underlying Python knowledge is HIGH-confidence, the "it is asked in a named T&I interview" claim is MEDIUM.
